Track 1 · Module 1 · Theory

The Consultative Mindset

Most reps lose deals before they even start — by pitching when they should be listening. This module is about the mindset shift that separates average AEs from the top performers.

The problem with pitching

Picture this: you get a 30-minute call with a Finance Manager at a 90-person consultancy. You spend the first 15 minutes walking through Flowline's features — time tracking, invoicing automation, the Xero integration. You know your product. You're confident.

The buyer says "interesting, we'll have a think" and goes quiet. You follow up three times. Nothing.

What went wrong? You answered questions they never asked. You solved problems you assumed they had. You talked about Flowline — not about them.

The core shift

A transactional seller asks: "How do I convince them to buy?"
A consultative seller asks: "What problem do they need solved, and am I the right person to solve it?"

What buyers actually want

Research by Gartner found that B2B buyers spend only 17% of their total purchase journey actually talking to sales reps. They've done research. They've formed opinions. By the time they're on a call with you, they don't want a product pitch — they want someone who understands their specific situation well enough to help them make a confident decision.

The consultative seller becomes a trusted advisor. They ask better questions. They listen more than they talk. They make the buyer feel understood — not sold to.

❌ Transactional
"Flowline integrates with Xero and automatically generates invoices, which saves your team hours every week."
✓ Consultative
"Walk me through how your team currently handles invoicing — where does it tend to break down for you?"

The 70/30 rule

In a consultative discovery call, the buyer should be talking 70% of the time. You talk 30% — and most of that is asking questions. If you find yourself presenting in the first 15 minutes, you've moved too fast.

Your job in discovery isn't to be impressive. It's to be curious.

Key principle: The best discovery calls end with the buyer saying "this is exactly what I needed to talk through" — before you've mentioned a single feature. That's the consultative mindset working.

The consultative mindset in action

Watch how a Flowline AE opens a call with Sarah Patel, Finance Manager at a 90-person consultancy. Notice what they don't do in the first 5 minutes.

AE — You (Flowline)
Thanks for the time, Sarah. Before I say anything about Flowline — can I ask what made you take this call? What's on your radar right now?
✓ Buyer talks first. AE stays curious, not promotional.
Sarah Patel — Finance Manager
Honestly our month-end process is a mess. We're manually pulling data from three systems and it takes forever. Someone sent me your LinkedIn post about invoice automation.
AE
Three systems — that sounds painful. What does that process actually look like from your end? Walk me through a typical month-end.
✓ Resists the urge to pitch. Goes deeper into the problem.
Sarah
So we've got our project management tool, then a separate time tracker that half the team doesn't use properly, and then our finance team manually builds the invoice in Excel. It usually takes 5 days and we almost always find errors after we've sent.
AE
Errors that have already gone to clients — that's a different kind of problem. What happens when that occurs? What does it cost you beyond just fixing the number?
✓ Moves from problem to implication — the real pain beneath the surface
Sarah
It's embarrassing, honestly. We had one client query a £12k discrepancy last quarter. They paid in the end but the relationship took a hit. And I've got two analysts who spend half their week on this instead of doing actual analysis.
AE
So the numbers are one thing, but it sounds like what's really at stake is client trust and what your team's time is worth. Is that a fair summary of what you'd want to fix?
✓ Reflects back their pain in their words — buyer feels understood
Notice: The AE hasn't mentioned a single Flowline feature. They've spent the whole conversation helping Sarah articulate her own problem. That's the consultative mindset — and Sarah is now far more invested in finding a solution.
